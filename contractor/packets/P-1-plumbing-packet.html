P-1 Plumbing · 64 Stone Sheep Circle

Fixtures from A-5; one drain tree out the rear wall at 25,0, and group PEX feeds. A first draft for the plumber (slab on grade). App version v23. From the owner's plans (Larson House, 6/11/26).

Notes

  1. Slab on grade assumed (A-5 shows floor drains in the garage and mechanical closet). All drains and supply runs are under the slab or in walls; no water lines in the attic.
  2. One drain tree, every line falling to where the building drain leaves the house at 25,0 (the rear wall line) at an invert of 2'-6 1/2" below the finished floor: a 4" building drain straight out the rear at X 25, a 3" east trunk at Y 26.8 for the kitchen and both east baths, a 3" master bath branch at Y 12, and a 2" line from the garage and mechanical floor drains. 41 DFU in all.
  3. Slopes are the IPC minimums (1/8" per ft at 3" and 4", 1/4" per ft below 3"), worked back from the exit; the shallowest invert is 1'-5 1/2" under the slab.
  4. Under the slab, top down: 4" of concrete; R-1's 5" nub boards with the radiant loops on them (9" to their underside); R-1's 1" feed pairs right under the boards; then P-1 in the base course, each in its own band so nothing crosses at the same depth: cold PEX about 1'-0" down, hot PEX about 1'-1 1/2" down, and every drain below both (crowns no higher than 1'-2 1/2"). Vent legs under the slab leave their drain at its own depth. Rough in, test and backfill the plumbing before the vapor barrier and boards go down; sleeve each line where it comes up through the boards and slab.
  5. Supply: 1" service to a main manifold in the mechanical closet, then one cold and hot pair to each fixture group, in one piece under the slab (no fittings under the slab), to a small manifold in the vanity or wet wall; short 1/2" lines from there to each fixture. 724 ft of PEX in all. 25.3 cold + 10.4 hot WSFU. Hot runs under the slab get R-3 closed-cell insulation (IECC R403.5.3), since they sit below the radiant boards. Pressure-balanced or thermostatic valves at the tubs and showers.
  6. Hot water takes longest at master bath (about 47 s at a lavatory); see open items for a recirculation pump.
  7. Every trap is vented: up the wall behind the fixture and across the attic to its group's 3" stack, which goes through the roof. Wet walls framed 2x6 (see S-1). ADA toilets as drawn: 17-19" bowl height, grab-bar blocking.
  8. Water heater in the mechanical closet with a pan and the T&P and pan drain to the closet floor drain.

Open items

  • Confirm the drain leaves at 25,0 (assumed): city sewer or septic, and the invert there. The house needs about 2'-6 1/2" of cover at the wall plus the fall to the main or tank.
  • Water source (city or well) and where it enters; the main manifold is drawn in the mechanical closet.
  • A demand recirculation pump (button or motion sensor at each bath) on the hot side would cut the wait at the far baths to a few seconds; it needs a 1/2" return or the cold line as the return.
  • Water heater fuel and type. A heat-pump water heater needs about 700 cu ft of air or a ducted kit; the 3' x 8' mechanical closet is too small without one. A gas unit needs venting and combustion air.
  • Powder room sink is not drawn on A-5 (one is shown here, assumed).
  • Garage floor drain: sanitary with an interceptor, or to daylight (lot grading).
  • Kitchen island vent method (loop vent vs. AAV) depends on the local code.
  • Fixture models: A-5 tags the drains K-9135 and K-9136; tub, toilet, faucet and valve models still to pick.
