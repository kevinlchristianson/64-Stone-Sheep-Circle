R-1 Radiant · 64 Stone Sheep Circle

PEX loops in the slab, four thermostat zones, manifolds and flows at Powell, WY design (-11°F). App version v9. From the owner's plans (Larson House, 6/11/26).

Notes

  1. Positions are taped from the nearest wall face, exterior walls first. Every panel stops 6" off the walls, so interior wall plates fasten in that band; mark every tube crossing under a wall line on the vapor barrier and keep shots and anchors 12" from it.
  2. Tube: 1/2" O2-barrier PEX in counterflow spirals (supply in, return out between), tied to the mesh at mid-depth of a 4" slab. 8 loops run at 9" o.c. or 6" (baths, and rooms needing 15 Btu/h per sq ft or more; 6" in the dining room and Bath 1), the rest at 12". One continuous coil per loop, no fittings in the slab. Tag both ends of every loop at its manifold.
  3. No tube under the kitchen base cabinets, island, fridge or back cabinet, the tubs, the vanities, the laundry cabinets or the fireplace hearth. Keep tube 6" from toilet flanges and 12" from floor and shower drains; the showers' floors are heated.
  4. Under the slab: R-10 (2") rigid foam under the whole heated slab and the garage slab, with R-10 at the slab edge (IECC 2021 zone 6 for a heated slab), and a 10-mil vapor barrier over the foam.
  5. Zoning is by thermostat: four zones (master suite, great room and utility, bedroom wing and office, garage) on 24 V actuators at each loop's manifold port, so a room can get its own thermostat later by rewiring, without new tube.
  6. Manifolds, three stations: MF-1 (laundry, on the north wall between the linen alcove and the dryer) serves Master Bed, Master Bath, Master Closet, Master Hall, Laundry, Powder, Dining; MF-2 (garage, on the mud room wall, beside MF-G) serves Mud Room, Kitchen, Pantry, Entry; MF-G (garage, on the mud room wall, beside MF-2) serves Garage; MF-3 (bedroom hall, recessed in the west wall north of the fireplace) serves Kitchen, Living, Office, Bedroom Hall, Bedroom 1, Bath 1, Closet (Bedroom 2), Bedroom 2, Bath 2, Bedroom 3, Closet (Bedroom 3). HS-1 in the mechanical closet feeds all three.
  7. Leaders run in the slab as tight pairs (drawn spread out). Sleeve them in 3/8" foam where they pass under another zone (136' in all) and wherever they cross a sawcut control joint. Feed pairs from HS-1 to the three stations are 1" PEX, insulated end to end, 122' each way in all.
  8. Flow: 10.9 gpm with every loop calling, each loop set on its flow meter at a 10°F drop. Design heat 54,493 Btu/h, of which 10,834 is the garage at 50°F; 10% is added for heat lost down and out the edge.
  9. Water: 110°F supply at -11°F outside, reset lower as it warms, so any low-temperature source can run it. Floor surfaces stay at or under 90°F. At that supply L14 (Closet (Bedroom 2), Bath 1) falls about 135 Btu/h short at design; see the open items.
  10. Pump: the hardest loop is L4 at 8.0 ft in the tube; add about 3 ft for the feed pair, manifold and actuator. A constant-ΔP ECM circulator at about 12 ft covers it.
  11. Pressure test every loop at 100 psi water or 60 psi air for 24 hours before the pour and keep it under pressure through the pour. Photograph the layout with a tape in frame before concrete.

Open items

  • L14 (Closet (Bedroom 2), Bath 1): the floor carries all but about 135 Btu/h of the room at -11°F with 110°F water. Add a small electric towel warmer, or run the boiler hotter on the coldest days.
  • Boiler model: HS-1 is a condensing boiler on natural gas or propane (decided 2026-10-13). Pick a combi or a boiler with an indirect tank if it should also make the domestic hot water and replace P-1's 50 gal heater; propane needs the boiler's LP conversion kit.
  • Garage: kept at 50°F on its own manifold, pump and thermostat. Decide whether it runs a glycol mix behind a plate heat exchanger, which protects it if the house loses power in a cold snap with the doors open.
  • Flooring: the loops assume hard-surface floors. Carpet in a bedroom needs about 10°F warmer water for that loop, or 9" spacing there.
  • Mechanical closet: HS-1, pump P-R1 and the zone controller share it with P-1's water manifold and water heater; that is why no manifold is drawn there (O5 in the options shows the fourth manifold saves little tube). A combi boiler removes the water heater and makes room.
  • MF-3 cabinet depth in the hall's 2x4 west wall (S-1), and the boiler, pump and zone controller circuits (E-1).
